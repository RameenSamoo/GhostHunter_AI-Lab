## 1. Project Motivation

In the AI lab we studied search, constraint satisfaction, probabilistic reasoning, and intelligent agents one topic at a time. **Ghost Hunter 2D** unites all of them in a single playable Python/Pygame simulation. An invisible ghost moves stochastically across a maze while emitting only noisy walking-distance readings and brief visibility flickers. A Hidden Markov Model (HMM) maintains a live belief heatmap over the grid, while an autonomous AI opponent (*Sir Nadeem*) hunts the same ghost under identical rules so human players can compete directly against a probabilistic agent.

---

## 2. Objective

1. **CSP Maze Generation:** Generate connected, fair mazes with exact wall counts and minimum spawn distance.
2. **HMM Ghost Tracking:** Infer the hidden ghost's location from noisy BFS-distance readings and random-walk motion.
3. **BFS & A* Search:** Precompute walking distances with BFS and plan shortest pursuit/hint paths with A*.
4. **Autonomous Agent (Sir Nadeem):** Build a goal-based AI hunter obeying human speed limits and partial observability.
5. **Persistence & Benchmarking:** Store profiles/leaderboards in SQLite and evaluate all components quantitatively.

## 3. Datasets and Simulation Environment

The project does not rely on an external static CSV dataset; instead, the simulation generates reproducible seeded data across four categories (visualized in `GhostHunter2D_Evaluation.ipynb`):

| Data Type | How It Is Produced | Used For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mazes | Generated by CSP from a random seed (`8x8` to `25x25`, `10–20%` walls). | Game levels & CSP benchmarks |
| Ghost Movement | Uniform random walk among open neighbors (excluding active hunters). | Hidden state tracked by HMM |
| Sensor Readings | True BFS walking distance + Gaussian noise (`sigma = 1.5`), rounded `>= 0`. | Observation input to HMM |
| Saved Games | SQLite tables (`players`, `games`, `results`) in `ghost_hunter.db`. | Profiles, Top-5 scores & AI vs Human |

## 4. Model and Features Used

The core engine (`core/`) is decoupled from Pygame so every AI module can be simulated and tested headlessly.

### 4.1 PEAS Formulation

| Component | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| **Performance** | `Score = max(0, 10000 - 50*seconds - 20*moves) * multiplier - 500*radar - 300*hint` |
| **Environment** | Partially observable, stochastic, dynamic, discrete 2D grid maze |
| **Actuators** | Cardinal moves (`UP`, `DOWN`, `LEFT`, `RIGHT`, `None`) capped at `6.0 cells/s` |
| **Sensors** | Own cell, noisy BFS distance reading (`sigma = 1.5`), `0.3s` ghost flickers, partner cell |

### 4.2 Level Generation (CSP)

Each level is solved as a two-stage CSP (`core/csp.py`): Stage 1 places exact wall counts in shuffled order, running BFS after each wall to undo any placement that disconnects the floor. Stage 2 assigns hunter (`H`), optional VS partner (`P`, adjacent to `H`), and ghost (`G`) spawns via backtracking with forward checking so `Manhattan(H, G) >= floor(N/2)` and `Manhattan(P, G) >= floor(N/2)`.

### 4.3 Ghost Tracking (HMM) & Search (BFS / A*)

`BeliefTracker` (`core/hmm.py`) maintains a normalized probability vector over all walkable cells. On each ghost tick, it predicts via transition matrix `T` (`b <- normalize(b * T)`). On each reading, it updates via Gaussian likelihood `L(obs | g) = exp(-(obs - d_BFS(h, g))^2 / (2*sigma^2)) + eps` (`sigma = 1.5`, `eps = 1e-4`). Visited hunter cells are zeroed out (`exclude`), and flicker/radar sightings collapse belief to `1.0`. BFS precomputes all-pairs distances, while A* (Manhattan heuristic) plans shortest hint and AI paths.

### 4.4 Autonomous AI Opponent (Sir Nadeem) & Game Features

Sir Nadeem (`core/ai_agent.py`) runs a goal-based cycle every frame: *Perceive -> Update HMM -> Choose MAP Goal -> Replan A* (avoiding the other hunter) -> Act* at human speed caps. The ghost model (`core/grid.py`) excludes active hunter cells so it never self-captures on an idle player. Scoring follows `Score = max(0, 10000 - 50*seconds - 20*moves) * multiplier - 500*radar - 300*hint` across three modes (Solo, VS Simultaneous, VS Turn-Based).

| Difficulty | Grid | Walls | Ghost Tick | Flicker Gap | Multiplier | AI Speed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | 8 × 8 | 6 (10%) | 1.0 s | 5 – 8 s | 1.0× | 5.00 cells/s |
| Intermediate | 12 × 12 | 22 (15%) | 0.8 s | 7 – 10 s | 1.5× | 5.33 cells/s |
| Professional | 18 × 18 | 58 (18%) | 0.6 s | 9 – 13 s | 2.0× | 5.66 cells/s |
| Expert | 25 × 25 | 125 (20%) | 0.4 s | 11 – 16 s | 3.0× | 6.00 cells/s |

## 6. Experimental Results

### 6.1 CSP, Search, HMM & Baseline Benchmarks

| Difficulty | CSP Time (ms) | A* vs BFS Expanded | HMM Error / Hits | AI Catch Rate | Random Catch Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner (8×8) | 0.3 – 1.0 | 6.9 vs 30.0 | 2.6 cells / 26.6% | 100% (2.8s) | 99% (20.3s) |
| Intermediate (12×12) | 2.2 – 6.7 | 11.1 vs 60.2 | 3.5 cells / 23.5% | 100% (3.4s) | 79% (44.2s) |
| Professional (18×18) | 13.2 – 48.1 | 18.4 vs 132.1 | 4.5 cells / 21.6% | 100% (4.5s) | 58% (65.9s) |
| Expert (25×25) | 52.5 – 198.7 | 33.7 vs 253.9 | 5.8 cells / 17.3% | 100% (5.7s) | 25% (49.7s) |

A* matched BFS shortest-path lengths on 4,000/4,000 pairs while expanding 4–8× fewer cells. The HMM achieved 15–85× more exact hits than random guessing, enabling Sir Nadeem to catch 100% of ghosts vs. only 25% for a random-walk baseline on Expert.

### 6.2 AI vs. Human Performance Comparison & Testing

Using recorded human and AI matches from `ghost_hunter.db` (47 logged results), Sir Nadeem consistently outperforms human players across all difficulty tiers:

| Difficulty | AI Avg Score | Human Avg Score | AI Avg Time (s) | Human Avg Time (s) | AI Avg Moves | Human Avg Moves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | 9,606 | 9,554 | 3.30 | 5.01 | 11.4 | 9.8 |
| Intermediate | 14,438 | 9,408 | 3.30 | 68.43 | 10.5 | 15.3 |
| Professional | 18,105 | 16,186 | 6.05 | 21.82 | 32.3 | 40.8 |
| Expert | 27,675 | 23,409 | 4.57 | 20.10 | 27.3 | 59.6 |

- **Why AI Is Better:** Sir Nadeem updates all $N$ cell probabilities instantaneously via Bayes' rule, replans optimal A* paths every step (cutting Expert moves from 59.6 to 27.3), and never pauses or incurs Radar (-500) / Hint (-300) penalties—despite sharing the exact same speed cap and noisy percepts as the human.
- **Automated Test Suite:** All **130/130** `pytest` unit tests pass across CSP (22), search (5), HMM (5), scoring (5), session (10), VS/Sir Nadeem (40), database (25), profile flow (10), and UI smoke (8).

## 9. Conclusion

Ghost Hunter 2D integrates CSP maze generation, BFS/A* search, HMM probabilistic tracking, and a fair goal-based AI agent (Sir Nadeem) into one cohesive system. Every generated maze satisfied all constraints, A* expanded 4–8× fewer nodes than BFS, the HMM localized the ghost far above chance, and Sir Nadeem achieved a 100% catch rate while outperforming recorded human runs across every difficulty tier.
